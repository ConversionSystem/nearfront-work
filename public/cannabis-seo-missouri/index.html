A fixed denominator

Why does Missouri have so few dispensaries for its sales?

Because the number of licenses is set by the state constitution rather than by demand, and the state has been issuing almost none. The DHSS annual report counts every new license and certification issued in program year 2024 at 71, and only seven of those were full dispensary licenses. One cultivation license. One manufacturing license. The rest of the volume came from the microbusiness program.

Set that against the sales line in the same report: $1.26 billion of adult-use retail in the year, and $2.2 billion cumulative since adult-use sales began in February 2023. Missouri routed $72.3 million of adult-use sales tax to the Veterans, Health and Community Reinvestment Fund and $7.4 million of medical sales tax to the Veterans Fund.

For an operator this produces an unusual competitive shape. The threat that dominates planning in open markets, a better-funded competitor opening nearby next quarter, mostly does not exist here. What replaces it is a zero-sum fight for trips among a known set of stores, which is decided by who customers find and choose rather than by who expands fastest.

In short: Missouri's license count is constitutionally capped, and the state issued only seven new full dispensary licenses in program year 2024 out of 71 new licenses and certifications total. Against that fixed field it sold $1.26 billion of adult-use cannabis in the year and $2.2 billion cumulatively since February 2023.

Where the stores are

Which Missouri cities have the most dispensaries?

Kansas City leads with 23 and Saint Louis city follows with 22, so the two metros hold about a fifth of the state's dispensaries between them. After that the list flattens fast: Springfield 9, Columbia 8, Joplin 5, Saint Peters 5, Cape Girardeau 5, Independence 4, Florissant 3 and Branson 3.

That long tail is the interesting part. Once past the two big metros, most Missouri dispensary markets are three to nine stores serving a city and everything around it. In a five-store town the Map Pack is the whole competitive surface, because the top three results and the map are what a customer sees before deciding where to drive.

The list also shows how the constitutional cap distributes. Cape Girardeau, a small city near the Illinois line, holds as many dispensary licenses as Joplin and Saint Peters. Missouri does not publish where customers come from, so causes stay unstated, but the pattern says market size in this state is not a simple function of the population inside city limits.

In short: Kansas City has 23 licensed dispensaries and Saint Louis city 22, followed by Springfield 9, Columbia 8, then Joplin, Saint Peters and Cape Girardeau with five each. Beyond the two metros most Missouri dispensary markets contain fewer than ten stores, which makes the Map Pack the decisive competitive surface.

The only door still open

Is anyone new getting into the Missouri market?

Through one route: the microbusiness program. Of the 71 new licenses and certifications issued in program year 2024, 25 were microbusiness dispensary licenses and 33 were microbusiness wholesale licenses, which is 57 of the year's new licenses in a single lane. The report counts 96 microbusiness licenses since the program's inception.

That matters to an established dispensary for two reasons. It is where new local search competition will come from, since a microbusiness dispensary appears in the same results as a comprehensive one. And it is a signal about how the state intends to grow the market, by widening ownership rather than by adding conventional storefronts.

The established field, meanwhile, is not standing still on price or product, but it is standing still on address. In a market where the store list barely changes from year to year, the entire competitive question becomes which of those stores a customer sees first when they search, and that is a position built over months of consistent work rather than won in a campaign.

In short: Missouri's microbusiness program is the main entry route: 25 microbusiness dispensary and 33 microbusiness wholesale licenses were issued in program year 2024, 57 of the year's 71 new licenses, with 96 microbusiness licenses issued since inception. Conventional dispensary licensing has effectively stopped.
