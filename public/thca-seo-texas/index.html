Market shape

How many hemp retailers are there in Texas?

13,608 distinct addresses are registered for hemp retail, held across 10,989 registrations and 7,538 separate businesses, according to the Department of State Health Services list published on 14 July 2026. A further 753 businesses hold Consumable Hemp Product licenses to manufacture or process.

Those three numbers are not interchangeable and most published figures confuse them. A single registration can cover many locations, and 101 registration numbers repeat the same address across multiple rows in the state file, which inflates a naive row count. Addresses are the number that matters for local search, because each address is a potential Google Business Profile.

For scale: Oregon, one of the most saturated cannabis markets in the country, has 678 licensed retailers statewide. Texas hemp retail is roughly twenty times that. Houston alone carries 1,660 registered locations, more than double Oregon's entire cannabis sector.

In short: Texas has 13,608 distinct registered hemp retail addresses, across 10,989 registrations and 7,538 businesses, per the DSHS list of 14 July 2026, plus 753 licensed manufacturers. Houston holds 1,660 locations, San Antonio 1,140, Austin 966, Dallas 464 and Fort Worth 309.

Who actually holds the registrations

Why is Texas hemp retail a multi-location SEO problem?

Because the registration rolls are dominated by chains, not specialty shops. The largest single registration, number 4071, belongs to BW Gas & Convenience Retail LLC and covers 249 locations. The second, number 2127, is Spec's Family Corp with 224. These are convenience stores and liquor retailers, not head shops, and they are the structural reality of the category in Texas.

That changes the discipline entirely. Single-store local SEO is one Google Business Profile, one set of citations, one review stream. A 249-location operator has 249 profiles, 249 review streams, 249 sets of hours and categories, and a name-address-phone consistency problem that compounds across every directory Google reads. Most of the failures we see in multi-location retail are not ranking failures, they are data-integrity failures: duplicate profiles, wrong hours, stores mapped to the wrong side of a highway.

It also changes where an independent competes. A single-location retailer will not out-rank a 249-store chain on a statewide term and should not try. What an independent can win is the block, the neighborhood, and the specific product query, because those are the places where a chain's centrally managed profiles are thinnest and where genuine local relevance still decides the result.

In short: The largest Texas registration covers 249 locations under one number, held by a convenience chain, and the second covers 224. Multi-location operators face a data-integrity problem across hundreds of Google Business Profiles rather than a ranking problem, while independents win at neighborhood and product level where centrally managed chain profiles are weakest.

The regulatory position

What does Texas law currently say about hemp products?

This section reports what the Department of State Health Services publishes, with dates, and nothing beyond it. Hemp regulation in Texas is changing quickly and the accurate position today may not be the position next quarter.

DSHS states that consumable hemp products may contain delta-9 tetrahydrocannabinol at a concentration of not more than 0.3 percent on a dry weight basis, under Texas Health and Safety Code 443. On delta-8, DSHS points to the Texas Supreme Court decision in Texas Department of State Health Services v. Sky Marketing Corp, which confirmed that delta-8 THC is a controlled substance and that only naturally occurring trace amounts in the hemp plant are exempt. DSHS states that the Texas schedule affecting delta-8 THC became effective on 31 July 2026, and that products labeled to contain delta-8, or shown to contain more than trace amounts of tetrahydrocannabinols including delta-8, may be detained and referred to law enforcement.

Separately, DSHS adopted emergency rules on 2 October 2025 prohibiting sales of consumable hemp products to anyone under 21 and requiring valid government-issued identification before purchase, with permanent revocation available as a penalty. Registration costs $5,000 per location per year and a manufacturing license costs $10,000 per location per year, both set out in 25 TAC 300.

One thing we will not do is tell you where THC-A sits in that framework. The DSHS Consumable Hemp Program page does not use the terms THC-A, THCA, tetrahydrocannabinolic acid, or total THC anywhere. It addresses delta-8 and delta-9 by name. Any confident public claim about THC-A's status in Texas is going beyond what the state has actually published, and we are not going to make one on a marketing page. Read the DSHS program page directly and take your own legal advice.

In short: DSHS permits consumable hemp products with no more than 0.3 percent delta-9 THC by dry weight, treats delta-8 THC as a controlled substance with a schedule effective 31 July 2026, and since 2 October 2025 requires 21-plus sales with government ID. Registration is $5,000 per location per year. DSHS does not address THC-A by name anywhere on its program page, so no reliable public claim about THC-A's Texas status can be drawn from it.

What does it cost to sell hemp products in Texas?

Both figures are stated by DSHS and set out in 25 TAC 300. Because the fees are per location, a large chain's cost base differs enormously from a single store's.
