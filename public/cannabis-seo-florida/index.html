Consolidation, in one table

Who actually operates Florida's dispensaries?

A short list. The state's weekly update publishes a table of every Medical Marijuana Treatment Center with the number of dispensing locations each one runs, and the distribution is steep: Trulieve 170, MUV 86, Curaleaf 75, Ayr 65, Surterra 44, Green Dragon 41, Planet 13 34, FLUENT 33, Sunnyside 31, Sanctuary 27. Twenty-five operators appear in the table, three of them with no locations at all.

Trulieve alone holds 21.9 percent of the state's dispensing locations. The top four hold 396 of 776, or 51.0 percent. There is no other large cannabis market in the country where the leader operates one storefront in five.

Florida's license structure is why. An MMTC license is vertically integrated, covering cultivation, processing and retail together, so scale compounds inside a single company rather than spreading across independents. For a smaller operator that changes what competing means: you are not going to out-open Trulieve, and the question becomes which patients can find you first in the places you already are.

In short: Florida's weekly state update lists 25 Medical Marijuana Treatment Centers operating 776 dispensing locations. Trulieve runs 170 of them, 21.9 percent, and the four largest operators run 396 between them, 51.0 percent. MMTC licenses are vertically integrated, which concentrates scale inside single companies.

Nearly a million cardholders

How big is Florida's medical cannabis patient base?

939,639 people held an active medical cannabis ID card as of 21 August 2026. That is a patient population larger than the entire adult-use customer base of several legal states, and every one of those people had to see a qualified physician and register with the Department of Health to get there.

The volume moving through those 776 locations is published weekly. In the seven days from 14 to 20 August, Florida MMTCs dispensed 439,096,212 milligrams of THC and 152,157 ounces of marijuana in a form for smoking. The state reports these as quantities rather than sales, so anyone quoting a Florida market size in dollars is quoting an estimate from somewhere else.

The registered nature of that audience changes the search picture in a way most cannabis marketing ignores. These are repeat customers with a documented condition and a card that expires, which means the valuable queries are not only variations of dispensary near me but the practical ones: which locations carry which products, what a card renewal involves, and which nearby MMTC has the thing a patient actually uses.

In short: Florida had 939,639 patients with active medical cannabis ID cards as of 21 August 2026, served by 776 dispensing locations. In the week of 14 to 20 August those locations dispensed 439,096,212 mg of THC and 152,157 ounces of smokable product. The state publishes quantities, not revenue.

Where the small operator wins

How does a smaller Florida MMTC compete for patients?

Not on footprint, and not on advertising. Google and Meta both prohibit paid promotion of THC products, so the operator with 170 locations cannot buy visibility either. What it has instead is presence: more storefronts means more Google Business Profiles, more review histories, and more chances to be the closest result.

The counterweight available to everyone else is precision. A large chain manages hundreds of profiles centrally and generically. A single location that keeps its hours, categories, product attributes and photos genuinely current, answers reviews the same week, and publishes real detail about what is on its shelves will beat a chain's neglected listing in its own neighborhood more often than its size suggests.

The second lever is the content nobody at scale bothers to write. Patients in a registered program ask specific, answerable questions about cards, renewals, forms of product and what to bring. Those questions have documented answers, they are searched, and a page that answers them properly earns visits that no amount of storefront count can intercept.

In short: Since paid THC advertising is prohibited on Google and Meta, large Florida operators compete on storefront density rather than media spend. Smaller MMTCs win locally through precise, current Google Business Profiles, weekly review response, and content answering the specific questions a registered patient population searches for.
