A billion dollars through few doors

How big is the Phoenix cannabis market?

Arizona's Department of Revenue puts adult-use taxable sales at $1,068,498,368 for fiscal 2026, on top of $1.05 billion the year before, with $5.25 billion cumulative since sales began in January 2021. The state collected $268.9 million in marijuana taxes in FY2026, including $167.3 million of excise.

What makes those numbers unusual is the denominator. The health department counted 170 adult-use establishments statewide as of August 2026. California moves more total dollars, but it does so through thousands of licenses. Arizona pushes a billion-dollar market through fewer stores than some single metro areas hold elsewhere, and the state's list has grown from 143 licensees in mid-2021 to 170 establishments in five years.

Phoenix is the center of gravity: 47 of the 181 facilities on the state's live lookup, ahead of Tucson's 23 and Mesa's 17. Maricopa County also holds 44,045 of the state's 70,444 active medical patients. High demand, few competitors, slow entry: the economics of every ranking position are amplified accordingly.

In short: Arizona did $1.07 billion in adult-use taxable sales in FY2026 and $5.25 billion cumulative since January 2021, through only 170 establishments statewide as of August 2026. Phoenix holds 47 of the 181 facilities on the state's live lookup, the largest count of any Arizona city.

The list the state stopped publishing

How many dispensaries does Phoenix actually have?

Officially harder to answer than it should be. The health department's last published establishment list is dated June 30, 2021, when Phoenix held 38 of 143 licensees. It has not been updated since. The current source of truth is AZ Care Check, the department's live lookup, which listed 47 marijuana facilities in Phoenix when we queried it in August 2026, every one of them operating.

The five-year comparison is worth noticing: Phoenix went from 38 to 47 listed facilities while statewide count went from 143 to about 180. Roughly a quarter of Arizona's marijuana retail has sat in Phoenix the whole time. Entry is slow, and the players mostly stay the players: Trulieve alone operates multiple Phoenix locations, alongside names like JARS Cannabis, Ponderosa and TruMed.

For an operator, the practical consequence is the same one every slow-entry market produces. Your competitive set is stable and known. Growth comes from taking search visibility, foot traffic and repeat orders from the other 46, not from outrunning a wave of new openings. That is a marketing problem with a known shape, and it rewards sustained work over stunts.

In short: The state's last published establishment list, dated June 2021, gave Phoenix 38 of 143 licensees. Its live lookup, AZ Care Check, listed 47 Phoenix marijuana facilities in August 2026, all operating, the most of any Arizona city. Entry is slow and the competitive set is stable.

Medical is fading here too

Should Phoenix dispensaries still market to medical patients?

The trend line says the adult-use shelf is where the market went. Medical taxable sales fell from $214.1 million in FY2025 to $132.0 million in FY2026, a 38 percent drop in one year, while adult-use held above a billion. Active patient cards stand at 70,444 statewide, most of them in Maricopa County.

The department still counts 136 medical dispensaries statewide, and the patient base remains real, loyal and high-frequency. But a Phoenix store building its growth plan on the medical segment is building on a number that just shrank by more than a third in twelve months, per the state's own tax file.

The search behavior follows the money. The volume is in adult-use queries: dispensary near me, deals, brands and product categories, asked by people who have never held a card. Winning those searches across the sprawl of Phoenix, from the central corridor to Ahwatukee to Deer Valley, is a different and larger job than holding a medical patient list, and it is the one that matches where the dollars moved.

In short: Arizona's medical taxable sales dropped 38 percent in one year, $214.1M in FY2025 to $132.0M in FY2026, while adult-use exceeded $1 billion. Medical patients remain a real but shrinking segment; the growth marketing job in Phoenix is adult-use search visibility.
