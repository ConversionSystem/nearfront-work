The flattest field in Florida

How many dispensaries does Orlando have?

Forty-three inside the city, which is the largest count of any Florida city, ahead of Tampa's 36, Jacksonville's 35, and Miami's 19. Orange County as a whole holds 51, with Winter Park adding four, Apopka three and Clermont one.

The operator spread is what separates Orlando from the rest of the state. Trulieve holds six of the city's locations, Ayr five, Curaleaf four, then FLUENT and MUV three each, and Cookies two. No operator holds even a seventh of the city, in a state where the largest company runs 170 locations and the top four hold 51 percent of everything.

That flatness is the opportunity. In a market where one chain owns the map, the fight is for scraps of proximity. In Orlando the map is genuinely contested, which means positioning, reviews and profile quality decide outcomes that store count would otherwise settle.

In short: Orlando has 43 medical cannabis dispensing locations, the most of any Florida city, with 51 across Orange County. The largest operator holds six of them, and no company holds more than a seventh of the city, making Orlando the least concentrated large market in a heavily consolidated state.

Registered, and repeat

Who is actually searching for a dispensary in Orlando?

People who already hold a card. Florida is a medical-only market with 939,639 qualified patients holding an active ID card statewide, and every one of them was entered into the state registry by a qualified physician before they could buy anything.

That changes the shape of local search. A registered patient is a repeat customer with a renewal date, a preferred product form and a practical set of questions, so the valuable queries run past dispensary near me into which nearby location actually has a given product, what a renewal involves, and which store is open late on a Sunday.

It also means proximity matters more than brand. With 43 locations inside one city, most patients have several within a short drive, and the store they pick is usually the one that appears first with the right details attached rather than the one with the most recognizable name.

In short: Florida's cannabis market is medical only, with 939,639 patients holding active ID cards statewide. Orlando's searchers are registered repeat customers with renewal dates and product preferences, so practical queries about stock, hours and renewals matter alongside proximity searches.

What decides 43 near-identical listings

How does an Orlando dispensary get chosen?

By being findable with the right information attached, because almost everything else is equalized. Google and Meta both prohibit paid promotion of THC products, so no Orlando operator can buy its way to the top of a result page regardless of budget.

What remains is unglamorous and it compounds. A Google Business Profile that is genuinely complete and current, with correct hours, categories, attributes and recent photos. Reviews collected every week rather than in bursts, and answered. Product and menu detail on your own domain rather than parked on a third-party menu host that collects the search value instead of you.

And then the neighborhoods. Orlando is spread out, and a patient in Winter Park, Kissimmee or east Orange County is not choosing between all 43 stores, only the handful they can reach conveniently. Pages built for those specific areas, with real detail rather than a swapped city name, are what turn a citywide count into a local advantage.

In short: With paid THC advertising prohibited and 43 comparable locations in one city, Orlando dispensaries are chosen on findability and detail: complete and current Google Business Profiles, weekly review generation, menu content on your own domain, and pages built for specific neighborhoods rather than the city as a whole.
