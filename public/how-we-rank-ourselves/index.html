The rule

If it cannot be traced to a customer action, it does not go in the report.

Most dispensary reporting is a ranking screenshot and a chart of impressions. Both can move without a single extra person walking through the door, which is why owners stop reading them by month three.

We report against what customers did. Google's own local ranking documentation names relevance, distance and prominence as the three factors, and there is no way to pay for a better local position, so a report made only of positions is describing an input rather than an outcome. The four lines below are what we send instead.

You can check the method before you hire us.

Every statistic on our market guides comes from a state agency's own machine-readable file and carries three dates: the period the file covers, the date the agency published it, and the date we read it. Where a state does not publish something, the page says so rather than estimating. That is the same sourcing standard we apply to your market before we touch anything.

Our playbook works the same way: thirteen guides on how dispensary local search actually functions, with every rule quoted from Google's own documentation and linked, including the places where Google publishes nothing and we say so.

And our agency comparisons name four competitors, date every claim, link each competitor's own material, and invite corrections. If reporting standards matter to you, those three things will tell you more than any case study.

Why we work this way

Because our clients can't buy ads.

Dispensaries come to us because paid channels are closed to them. Google and Meta both prohibit THC advertising, so there is no budget lever to pull when a month goes badly, and no way to buy the result back. That makes honest measurement the whole job rather than a reporting formality.

It also means the work compounds slowly and unevenly. Google's own guidance says some changes take effect in hours, others take several months, and not every change produces a noticeable result at all. A report that hides that is setting you up to fire the wrong person in month four.

FAQ

Common questions

What is actually in a monthly report?

Four lines. Profile actions, meaning calls, direction requests, website clicks and menu clicks. Map position sampled across your trade area rather than at your own address. Revenue from your point of sale, split by new and returning. Search demand by query family. In that order, every month.

Why are rankings not the headline?

Because a ranking is an input, not a result. Google states on its local ranking page that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, so a deck built on positions describes something you cannot buy and never measures the thing you actually wanted.

What will you not put in a report?

A guaranteed position, because Google says plainly that no one can guarantee a number one ranking. A vendor score presented as Google's. An impression count with nothing attached to it. And any number we cannot trace back to Google's own reporting, your point of sale, or a named agency file.

Do you use a rank tracker?

We use tools, and we label what they are. Google states that third-party tools have no access to its internal ranking data and that none of them is approved by Google, so a tracker output is our measurement rather than Google's. Presenting it as Google's is the thing we refuse to do.

How do you measure map position across a trade area?

By sampling a grid of points across the neighborhoods your customers come from, because Google measures distance from the person searching rather than from your store. Google publishes no grid metric of its own, so this number is ours and it is labeled that way.
