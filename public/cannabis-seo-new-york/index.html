The core difference

Why is cannabis SEO different in New York City?

Google ranks local results on relevance, distance, and prominence. In most of the country distance does a great deal of the sorting, because customers drive and a competitor four miles away is genuinely a different option. That is the model an Oregon or a Maryland strategy is built on.

New York City breaks it. With 145 licensed dispensaries in Brooklyn and 142 in Manhattan, a dense stretch of the city can hold several stores within a few minutes on foot. When every candidate is close, proximity stops separating them, and the ranking weight shifts onto the signals that do: how complete and accurate the profile is, how recent and plentiful the reviews are, and how consistently the business appears across the web.

The practical consequence is that the usual excuse disappears. In a spread-out market a store can blame its address for poor visibility. In Manhattan, the competitor outranking you is often on the next block, working from the same distance advantage you have. The difference is the work.

In short: distance is a Google local ranking input, but in a walking city with 145 licensed dispensaries in Brooklyn and 142 in Manhattan, nearly every store is close to the searcher. Proximity stops being a differentiator, so profile quality, review recency, and citation consistency decide the ranking.

Tax structure

What is the cannabis tax rate in New York, and what changed?

Adult-use cannabis carries 9 percent state tax plus 4 percent local, 13 percent combined, and the local share stays with the city, town, village, or county where the sale takes place.

The structure is newer than most people realize. Before 1 June 2024, New York taxed cannabis by potency, in cents per milligram of THC, at different rates for flower, concentrate, and edibles. That was replaced with a 9 percent distributor tax. Any guide still explaining New York cannabis tax in cents per milligram is describing a system that ended more than two years ago, which is a reasonable way to judge how carefully a source maintains its content.

In short: New York adult-use cannabis is taxed at 9 percent state plus 4 percent local, 13 percent combined. The previous potency-based tax, charged in cents per milligram of THC, was replaced by a 9 percent distributor tax on 1 June 2024.

New York's Office of Cannabis Management lists 664 active adult-use retail dispensary licenses, plus 451 conditional adult-use retail dispensary (CAURD) licenses and 477 microbusiness licenses, as of August 2026. These are licenses issued rather than stores currently open, and the gap between the two is meaningful in New York.

What is the cannabis tax rate in New York?

Which New York City borough has the most dispensaries?
