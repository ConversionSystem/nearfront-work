A county of two doors

How many Santa Clara County cities allow dispensaries?

Two. The state's ordinance file records 13 of Santa Clara County's 15 incorporated cities as prohibiting storefront cannabis retail. The exceptions are San Jose and Mountain View. Unincorporated Santa Clara County prohibits every commercial cannabis activity outright.

The names on the prohibiting list are the ones that make this market unusual. Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Palo Alto, Cupertino, Milpitas and their neighbors are dense, wealthy, and closed. Our sum of their populations comes to 772,002 residents, none of whom can buy legally in the city where they live.

Mountain View, the only other permitted city, has 88,533 residents. San Jose has 980,434. In practical terms one city holds the overwhelming majority of the county's legal retail capacity and sits adjacent to almost all of its closed demand.

In short: Thirteen of Santa Clara County's 15 cities prohibit storefront cannabis retail, and unincorporated Santa Clara County prohibits all commercial cannabis activity. Only San Jose, with 980,434 residents, and Mountain View, with 88,533, permit a storefront. Roughly 772,002 people live in the county's prohibiting cities.

The catchment

Why does San Jose have an unusually large cannabis trade area?

Because the alternative does not exist nearby. In most metros a customer inconvenienced by one store simply visits another a few miles away. In Santa Clara County there is no third option: a resident of Cupertino or Palo Alto who wants to buy from a licensed storefront is choosing between San Jose and Mountain View, and for most of the county San Jose is closer.

This produces a pattern worth naming plainly. Search demand for dispensaries is generated across the whole county, but it can only be satisfied in two places. The queries originate in cities where no business can ever answer them locally, which means the competition for those searches is limited to operators who are already several miles away and who mostly are not trying.

It also means the usual proximity logic of local search works in your favor rather than against you. Google will show the nearest relevant results to someone searching from Sunnyvale, and the nearest relevant results are in San Jose. What decides which San Jose store appears is not distance, since every candidate is roughly equally far, but the quality of the profile, the recency of the reviews, and whether the business has published anything that connects it to Sunnyvale at all.

In short: A resident of any of Santa Clara County's 13 prohibiting cities has only San Jose or Mountain View to choose from. Because every San Jose store is roughly equidistant from those communities, proximity stops separating competitors and profile quality, review recency and locally relevant content decide the ranking instead.

Working the county

Which communities should a San Jose dispensary target?

The closed cities first, and by name. Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Cupertino, Palo Alto, Milpitas, Campbell, Los Gatos and Saratoga each generate their own search demand, and none of them will ever host a competitor who can undercut you on distance. Building genuine relevance for those communities is the highest-return work available in this market, and almost nobody does it because the instinct is to optimize for the city on your own signage.

Within San Jose itself, treat the city as several markets rather than one. It covers a large footprint from Willow Glen and Downtown out through Berryessa, Almaden and Evergreen, and a customer in one district is not realistically choosing a store in another. Measure position by district and stop averaging.

The one caution: relevance has to be real. A page that lists neighboring city names without saying anything true or useful about them is the thin-content pattern Google has spent years demoting, and it is what most programmatic city pages amount to. Write about the actual journey, the actual ordinance situation, and the actual reason someone from that city is in your store, or do not publish the page.

In short: Target the 13 prohibiting cities by name, starting with Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Cupertino and Palo Alto, because none can host a nearer competitor. Inside San Jose, measure and target by district rather than citywide. Content for neighboring cities must say something genuinely true about them or it becomes the thin-page pattern Google demotes.

San Jose, for context

San Jose is the seat of Santa Clara County, supported by the San Jose Chamber of Commerce and promoted by Visit San Jose. Its commercial districts run through Downtown, Santana Row, Willow Glen, Japantown and Berryessa, near destinations including the Tech Interactive and the San Jose Museum of Art. Customers here identify with their district and with the neighboring city they drove in from, and their searches reflect both.

FAQ

How many Santa Clara County cities allow dispensaries?

Can you buy cannabis in Sunnyvale, Cupertino or Palo Alto?

Not from a licensed storefront. All three are recorded as prohibiting storefront cannabis retail in the state's ordinance file, along with ten other Santa Clara County cities. Residents of those cities who want to buy from a licensed store are choosing between San Jose and Mountain View.

Why is San Jose's cannabis trade area larger than the city?

Because roughly 772,002 people live in Santa Clara County cities where no dispensary may lawfully open, and San Jose is the nearest permitted city for most of them. Demand is generated across the whole county but can only be met in two places, so a San Jose store draws from communities well beyond its own boundaries.

What is the cannabis excise tax in San Jose?

California charges 15 percent of gross receipts on retail sales, effective 1 October 2025. The rate had risen to 19 percent on 1 July 2025 before Assembly Bill 564 restored 15 percent that October. San Jose sets its own local cannabis business tax separately, so confirm the current rate with the city.
