The jurisdiction problem

Why do Baltimore City and Baltimore County have different dispensary counts?

Because they are different places, and Maryland is one of the few states where that sentence needs saying. Baltimore City is an independent city. It belongs to no county at all, and Baltimore County is a separate government whose seat is in Towson. The state's own dispensary file lists them on separate lines: Baltimore City 11, Baltimore 20. Anyone who groups that file by county and reads the larger number as the city has overstated the local field by roughly double and pointed the whole analysis at the wrong twenty businesses.

The trap is sharper than a naming quirk, because the addresses do not resolve it either. Two of the county's dispensaries carry Baltimore mailing addresses and the word Baltimore in their trade names: one on Rolling Mill Road, one on North Point Boulevard, both in ZIP 21224. A third store in that same ZIP, on Eastern Avenue, genuinely is inside the city. One postal code, one city name on the envelope, two sides of a government line.

That matters commercially rather than academically. Those two county stores compete in city searches, carry the city name in their branding, and will appear in a Map Pack a customer standing downtown sees. They belong in your competitive set. The eighteen other county stores, in Pikesville, Towson, Cockeysville and elsewhere, mostly do not. A competitor list built from a county grouping gets both halves of that wrong at once.

In short: Baltimore City is an independent city belonging to no county. Maryland's dispensary file records Baltimore City at 11 and Baltimore County at 20 as separate rows. Two county stores carry Baltimore addresses and Baltimore trade names in ZIP 21224, which also contains a genuine city store, so neither the county field nor the mailing address alone identifies your real competitive set.

Coverage, not crowding

Where are Baltimore's dispensaries actually located?

Spread thin, and that is the defining fact of the market. The city's eleven stores occupy eight different ZIP codes, and no ZIP holds more than two. There is no dispensary row, no cluster, nothing resembling the concentrated retail strips that define older adult-use cities. Coverage runs from Mount Washington in the northwest down through the Falls Road corridor, across to Belair Road in the northeast, and along the waterfront from Federal Hill through Fells Point to Canton.

Read that distribution as an opportunity map rather than a census. Large parts of west and southwest Baltimore have no dispensary in their ZIP at all. The people living there still search, and when they do, the result is decided by whichever store has done the work to be legible to Google beyond its own doorstep. In a dense market proximity settles almost everything. Here it settles very little, which hands the advantage to whoever earns it.

In short: Baltimore's 11 dispensaries sit in 8 ZIP codes, with 21201, 21211 and 21230 holding two each and five other ZIPs holding one. No cluster exists, and much of west and southwest Baltimore has no dispensary in its ZIP, so search visibility rather than proximity decides most of the city.

Baltimore, for context

Baltimore is governed by the City of Baltimore and represented by the Greater Baltimore Committee, with cultural programming through the Baltimore Office of Promotion and the Arts. Its commercial districts run through Hampden, Mount Vernon, Federal Hill, Fells Point and Canton, near destinations including the National Aquarium and the Enoch Pratt Free Library. Residents here name their neighborhood before their city, and their searches do the same.

FAQ

Common questions about cannabis SEO in Baltimore

How many dispensaries are in Baltimore?

Baltimore City has 11 licensed dispensaries as of the Maryland Cannabis Administration's June 2026 data release. Baltimore County, a separate jurisdiction that surrounds the city, has 20 more. The two are recorded on separate lines in the state's own file and should never be combined into a single Baltimore figure.

Why do Baltimore City and Baltimore County have different dispensary counts?

Baltimore City is an independent city and belongs to no county, one of the few such jurisdictions in the United States. Maryland's dispensary file lists Baltimore City with 11 and Baltimore County with 20 as separate rows. Two of the county stores carry Baltimore mailing addresses and the word Baltimore in their trade names, so address data alone will not tell you which side of the line a store sits on.

Is Baltimore the largest cannabis market in Maryland?

No. By dispensary count Baltimore City ranks behind Baltimore County with 20, Montgomery County with 19, and Prince George's County with 13, and is level with Anne Arundel County at 11. Maryland's retail sector is distributed across the suburban counties rather than concentrated in the city.

What is the cannabis tax rate in Maryland?

Maryland charges a 12 percent sales and use tax on adult-use cannabis, raised from 9 percent effective 1 July 2025. There is no additional Baltimore City cannabis tax. Medical cannabis remains exempt from that rate for registered patients.

How should a Baltimore dispensary compete when there are only eleven stores?

The opportunity is ranking for the neighborhoods between stores, where the search happens and no dispensary has a proximity advantage.
