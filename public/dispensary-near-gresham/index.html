Honest geography

What the page says when the store is not in the suburb

Sample Dispensary PDX, our fictional client, sits in outer east Portland. The page says so in its first sentence: it is not inside Gresham city limits and it does not pretend otherwise. It is the nearest licensed option many Gresham customers actually use, roughly a fifteen-minute drive along Powell or Stark depending on where in Gresham you start, and the page's job is to be the most honest, most useful answer to a Gresham search rather than a fake local storefront.

That honesty is not decoration. Google's local systems key on real location, and customers who feel baited do not come back. The satellite pattern works when it answers the nearby searcher better than anyone else, and fails when it pretends.

In short: A satellite page states plainly where the store actually is, gives the real drive relationship to the suburb, and wins by being the most useful answer for that suburb's searches rather than by faking a presence inside it.

Sourced, like everything we ship

The Gresham picture, with sources

Gresham is a separate city inside Multnomah County, and that boundary is the single most useful fact on this page. Six retail licenses carry a Gresham address in the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission license file, checked 10 August 2026, and none of them are part of Portland's 231. A Gresham customer weighing options is choosing among six local stores plus the outer-east-Portland stores a short drive away, which is a very different market from the Portland-wide picture most reports describe.

Oregon charges a 17 percent state tax on recreational cannabis, and cities may add up to 3 percent with voter approval, per the Oregon Department of Revenue. On a real client page this section carries whichever of the state facts file's sourced numbers are relevant to the suburb, and nothing that is not sourced. A suburb with no citable facts gets no invented color.

In short: Every number on a satellite page comes from the same state facts files as our market guides, cited inline with dates. Gresham: six OLCC retail licenses, a separate market from Portland's 231, under Oregon's 17 percent state tax plus up to 3 percent local.

The block worth stealing properly

While you are in Gresham

The best idea in the satellite pattern belongs to a competitor, and we credit it on our comparison pages: a block of real local institutions that gives the page genuine local texture and honest outbound links. Our rule for running it is that every entity is real and every URL is verified the day the page ships. For Gresham: the City of Gresham, the Gresham Area Chamber of Commerce, the Gresham Historical Society, and the county's Multnomah County Library branches. No invented businesses, no scraped lists.

In short: The local block links real, verified institutions only, checked on ship day. Local texture earns trust when it is true and destroys it when it is generated.

About this demo

Is this a real dispensary page?

No. It is a working demonstration of the satellite pages Nearfront builds around client dispensary locations, filled with a fictional client so the pattern can be judged on a live page. Every market fact on it is real and sourced; the store is not.

How many dispensaries does Gresham actually have?

Six retail licenses carry a Gresham address in the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission's license file, checked 10 August 2026. Gresham is a separate city inside Multnomah County, so its six are not part of Portland's 231, a boundary most Portland-wide reports get wrong.

Why would a satellite page admit the store is in another city?

Because customers and Google both punish pretending. The pattern works by honestly answering what a nearby searcher wants to know, the real drive, the real local picture, the real rules, and being the most useful result for it, not by faking a presence inside the suburb.

What stops these pages becoming duplicate content?

A measured gate. Sibling satellite pages must stay under 10 percent 8-gram overlap, the same validator threshold our own market guides ship under, and two suburbs that cannot be written distinctly do not both get pages. We measured a competitor's satellite pair at 15.3 percent, which is where our tooling refuses to publish.
