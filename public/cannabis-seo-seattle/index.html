The number behind the number

Where in Seattle did the dispensaries close?

Mostly in four places. Seattle's 90 permanent closures are not spread evenly across the city. Seventeen of them sit in 98103, the ZIP that covers Fremont, Wallingford and Green Lake. Fifteen are in 98134, the industrial district south of downtown. Ten are in 98106, the Delridge side of West Seattle, and ten more in 98125 around Lake City. Those four ZIP codes hold 52 of the 90 closures, 58 percent of the total, and between them they keep 12 stores open today.

At the other end of the city the same file reads like a different market. All five licenses ever issued in 98178, where the south end of Rainier Valley runs into unincorporated King County, are still open. Belltown's 98121 has five open and one closed. South Lake Union and lower Queen Anne, 98109, four open and one closed. Ballard's 98107 kept three of five. Counting only ZIPs that ever held at least five licenses, the closure share runs from 91 percent in 98106 down to zero in 98178.

That spread is why the citywide figure misleads. Seattle's 63 percent closure rate is an average of a 98103 that lost 17 of 20 and a 98121 that lost one of six. The state file does not say why any store closed, and this page will not guess. What it does show is that the odds a Seattle dispensary faced came with a ZIP code attached, and that the twelve survivors in the four hardest-hit ZIPs now trade on ground where 52 licenses did not last.

In short: Seattle's 90 permanent dispensary closures concentrate in four ZIP codes, 98103 with 17, 98134 with 15, 98106 with 10 and 98125 with 10, which hold 58 percent of the total and keep 12 open stores between them. At the other extreme, 98178 has lost none of its five licenses, 98121 one of six and 98109 one of five. The state publishes the status of each license, not the reason it closed.

Distance is measured from the searcher

What does a Seattle dispensary search return when the nearest store is gone?

Whichever nearby store Google judges most relevant and prominent. Google's own guidance says local results are based primarily on relevance, distance and prominence, and describes distance as how far each result is from the location term in the search or, when there is none, from what Google knows about the searcher's location. Distance is measured from the person, not from the store. Our reading of the Map Pack sets out what Google says about each factor and what it leaves unsaid.

Apply that to the map above. A searcher in 98115 has no store in their ZIP. Google's distance calculation runs from where they stand, so the stores in 98125 to the north, 98105 to the south and 98103 to the west are all in contention, and none of them is the closest by default. Relevance and prominence decide it. Prominence, in Google's words, draws on information from across the web such as links, articles and directories, and Google review count and review score factor into it. Three stores in 98103 survived 17 closures; whether one of them is the store 98115 sees depends on that work.

That is the mechanism this page exists to make visible. When someone in an emptied ZIP searches, the result is decided on the two factors a store can work on. Review count and score are named by Google. Review recency and velocity are not, whatever a sales deck says, and our guide to dispensary reviews goes through what the policy permits and what it forbids.

Two of Google's three factors are yours to work on. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence, and measures distance from the searcher. In a ZIP with no open store, no dispensary is closest by default, so relevance and prominence, which Google ties to links, review count and review score, decide which of the surrounding stores appears for a dispensary near me search.
