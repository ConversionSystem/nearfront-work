What Bud Authority genuinely does well

Bud Authority's site is the largest and most disciplined publishing operation in this category, and pretending otherwise would tell you more about us than about them. When we checked on 24 August 2026, their sitemap held 396 URLs, of which 136 are flat cannabis-seo pages covering states and cities in one namespace. Their Arizona state page ran 2,713 words of visible text.

They have also invested early and heavily in AI search. Their site carries a 36-kilobyte llms.txt file, a fourteen-article AEO and GEO knowledge section, and separate content hubs for answer-engine and generative-engine optimization. That is real work, done before most of the industry knew the acronyms.

And they publish comparison pages of their own, against four competitors, which takes a certain confidence. As of the same check, their agency-alternatives page does not mention us, which is part of why this page exists.

In short: Bud Authority runs the biggest published footprint in cannabis SEO, 136 geo pages and a real AEO library with llms.txt, verified against their own sitemap on 24 August 2026. The scale is genuine and we say so without hedging.

The actual difference

How is Nearfront different from Bud Authority?

The difference we would defend in front of any client is sourcing. Our 18 geo pages are built by parsing the state's own machine-readable licensing files, and every statistic on them carries the agency name, the file, an as-of date and a retrieved date. A reader, a client, or an AI system can re-derive our numbers. On the Bud Authority state page we checked, market figures are presented without linked primary sources, so they may well be right, but a reader cannot verify them from the page.

That difference shows up in the markup too. Both sites now carry FAQPage schema. Ours also ships a Dataset node with citation arrays on every geo page, pointing at the state files the numbers came from, which is precisely the structure AI answer engines are built to trust. Their state page we checked carries no Dataset schema.

The last row is philosophical but measurable: we publish our own rankings every month at /how-we-rank-ourselves/, wins and losses both, because we think an SEO agency that will not show its own numbers is asking for faith it has not earned. We found no equivalent on their site.

The honest verdict

When should you choose Bud Authority, and when Nearfront?

Choose Bud Authority if raw published footprint in your state matters most to you, if you want the agency with the largest content library in the category, or if their productized AEO packages match how you like to buy. None of that is a backhanded compliment; it is what their site is best at.

Choose Nearfront if you want every claim on your market's page to survive a fact-check, if you want the same sourcing discipline applied to your own site's content, and if you want an agency that publishes its own scoreboard monthly. Our method is slower per page, which is why our number is smaller, and we think that trade is the point rather than the weakness.

Either way, insist on the same standard from whoever you hire: ask where each number on your proposal came from, and ask to see it re-derived. An agency that can do that in front of you will do it in your content too.

In short: Bud Authority is the right call for maximum published footprint and packaged AEO. Nearfront is the right call for content that traces to primary sources and an agency that publishes its own results monthly. Ask either of us to re-derive a number live before you sign.
