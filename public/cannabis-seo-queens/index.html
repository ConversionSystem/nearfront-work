The data anomaly

Why does Queens look like it has no dispensaries?

Run the obvious query against New York's public licensing data, grouping retail dispensary licenses by city, and the results give you Brooklyn, Manhattan, the Bronx, Staten Island, Rochester, Buffalo. No Queens. The borough is simply absent.

It is not absent, of course. The Office of Cannabis Management records Queens addresses under neighborhood names: Jamaica, Flushing, Long Island City, Astoria, Ridgewood, Rosedale, and thirty more. Add them together and Queens holds 128 licenses, third in the city behind Brooklyn and Manhattan and ahead of the Bronx and Staten Island combined.

We mention this partly because it is a trap worth knowing if you research your own market, and partly because it is not really an accident. Queens has never behaved as a single place. It is the borough where the neighborhood name does the work the borough name does elsewhere, and the state's own data collection quietly reflects that.

In short: New York's licensing data has no Queens row. The borough is recorded under 35 neighborhood names such as Jamaica, Flushing, Astoria, and Long Island City. Summed, Queens holds 128 retail dispensary licenses with 71 operationally active, making it the third-largest cannabis retail borough in New York City.

No neighborhood holds more than 11 percent of the borough's licenses. Compare that with Brooklyn, where the top eight zip codes account for roughly 45 percent of the borough, or Manhattan at 39 percent. Queens is the flattest cannabis retail distribution in New York City by a clear margin.

Distance compounds the effect. Astoria and Rosedale are separated by most of the borough and by very different journeys. A store in Long Island City is functionally in the Manhattan orbit; a store in Bayside or Little Neck is serving an area where people drive, and where trade areas behave more like suburban Long Island than like the rest of New York City. These are not variations on one market. They are different markets that share a borough name.

In short: Jamaica leads Queens with 14 licenses, then Flushing 13, Long Island City 11, and Astoria 10. No neighborhood exceeds 11 percent of the borough total, making Queens the flattest cannabis retail distribution in New York City, well below Brooklyn's 45 percent top-eight concentration.

Queens, for context

Queens is served by the Queens Borough President's office and the Queens Chamber of Commerce. Its commercial corridors run through Jamaica Avenue, Roosevelt Avenue, and Steinway Street, near destinations including the Museum of the Moving Image in Astoria, Queens Botanical Garden in Flushing, and Flushing Meadows Corona Park. Customers here identify by neighborhood first and borough second, and their searches say so.

FAQ

Common questions about cannabis SEO in Queens

How many dispensaries are in Queens?

128 retail dispensary licenses, of which 71 are recorded as operationally active by the New York Office of Cannabis Management as of August 2026. Reaching that figure requires summing 35 separate neighborhood entries, because the state's licensing data has no single Queens row.

Which Queens neighborhoods have the most dispensaries?

Jamaica leads with 14 licenses, followed by Flushing with 13, Long Island City with 11, and Astoria with 10. Ridgewood and Rosedale have 6 each, then Bayside, Rego Park, Ozone Park, and South Richmond Hill with 5 apiece. No single neighborhood holds more than 11 percent of the borough's licenses.

Why does Queens look like it has no dispensaries in state data?

Because the Office of Cannabis Management records Queens addresses under neighborhood names such as Jamaica, Flushing, Astoria, and Long Island City rather than under Queens. A query grouped on city name returns nothing for Queens and can wrongly suggest the borough is empty, when it actually holds the third-largest license count in New York City.

Should a Queens dispensary target the borough or the neighborhood?

The neighborhood, almost always. Customers search for Astoria or Flushing rather than Queens, the neighborhoods are far apart, and a store in Jamaica competes with almost none of the stores in Astoria. Borough-level targeting spreads effort across markets you cannot realistically win.

What is the cannabis tax rate in Queens?

The New York State rate applies: 9 percent state plus 4 percent local, 13 percent combined, with the local share staying in New York City. There is no Queens-specific rate.
