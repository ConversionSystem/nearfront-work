Supply, in public

Why is cannabis so cheap in Michigan?

The state publishes the supply side in enough detail to see the pressure. Alongside 836 retailer licenses, the July 2026 report counts 798 Class C grower licenses, the largest cultivation tier, plus 68 Class B, 9 Class A and 61 excess grower licenses. On 31 July there were 336,640 immature, 499,927 vegetative and 696,985 flowering adult-use plants active in the state, and 320,972 plants were harvested during that single month.

Set that against the price line in the same report: $58.95 for an average retail ounce of flower. For comparison the state's own medical figure that month was $78.86 an ounce, on a program now selling $311,726 statewide against adult-use's $270.5 million.

The CRA publishes the volumes and the prices. It does not publish an explanation, and neither will we. What matters commercially is not why the number fell but what it removes: at $58.95 an ounce there is no discount a store can run that a competitor cannot match by Friday, and no margin to fund the paid advertising that Google and Meta prohibit for THC anyway.

In short: Michigan's July 2026 report counts 798 Class C grower licenses plus 138 other grower and excess licenses, over 1.5 million active adult-use plants and 320,972 plants harvested in the month, against an average retail flower price of $58.95 an ounce. The state publishes the volumes and prices without explaining the relationship, and at that price no store can differentiate on discounting.

Not where you would guess

Where in Michigan is the money actually spent?

The CRA breaks July sales into five regions, and they do not line up with population. Southwest Michigan sold $89.9 million. East and Southeast, the largest, sold $101.4 million. Wayne County, which contains Detroit, sold $30.6 million. Upper Lower and the UP together sold $32.3 million, and Mid Lower $16.4 million. The five sum to the statewide total to the cent.

Read the Southwest figure again. That region did nearly three times Wayne County's volume, and by weight it moved 210,653 pounds against Wayne's 54,787. Southwest Michigan borders Indiana and runs along the corridor toward Illinois. The state does not publish where buyers come from, so we will not claim to know, but any operator planning a Michigan footprint should notice that the biggest weight in the state moves nowhere near its biggest city.

The municipal picture reinforces it. Michigan's Treasury paid FY2025 excise distributions on 860 licenses spread across just 234 municipalities, $54,017.10 for each eligible license and $93.8 million in total. Retail is not evenly available across Michigan; it is concentrated in the places that opted in, and the customers of every town that opted out are driving to one of them.

In short: Michigan's five sales regions in July 2026: East and Southeast $101.4 million, Southwest $89.9 million, Upper Lower and the UP $32.3 million, Wayne County $30.6 million, Mid Lower $16.4 million. Southwest moved 210,653 pounds against Wayne's 54,787. Licensed retail exists in only 234 municipalities statewide.

What is left to compete on

What actually differentiates a Michigan dispensary?

Three things, and none of them is price. Where you are, which the state's own regional data shows matters enormously. Who can find you, which is a search problem. And what people say about you once they have, which is a review problem.

The scale of the workforce says how seriously the field takes this: 41,257 people worked in Michigan's licensed cannabis businesses as of 31 July 2026. That is a real industry with real marketing budgets, and it is competing for the same searches with the same prohibited paid channels. The stores that pull ahead are the ones treating local search as infrastructure rather than a line item.

For a store in an opted-in town surrounded by opted-out ones, the job is reach: rank for the places customers actually start from, not just the town on your sign. For a store in a dense corridor, the job is preference: be the one of six nearby that a customer chooses, which reviews and profile completeness decide far more often than inventory does.

In short: With price compressed and paid THC advertising prohibited on Google and Meta, Michigan dispensaries differentiate on location, findability and reputation. The state's 41,257 cannabis employees compete for the same searches, so local search position and review depth decide which nearby store a customer picks.

How big is Michigan's cannabis market?

The Cannabis Regulatory Agency reported $270,539,568 in adult-use sales for July 2026 and $15.15 billion cumulative since the market opened. Licensed cannabis businesses employed 41,257 people as of 31 July 2026.

How many dispensaries are in Michigan?

836 active adult-use retailer licenses statewide as of 31 July 2026, alongside 87 medical provisioning centers. The Michigan Treasury counted 860 retail and microbusiness licenses eligible for excise distributions as of 30 September 2025, spread across 234 municipalities.

What does cannabis cost in Michigan?

The state's July 2026 report puts the average retail adult-use flower price at $58.95 an ounce. The medical figure in the same report is $78.86 an ounce.

Which part of Michigan sells the most cannabis?

East and Southeast Michigan led with $101.4 million in July 2026, followed by Southwest at $89.9 million. Wayne County, which contains Detroit, recorded $30.6 million. By weight Southwest moved 210,653 pounds against Wayne's 54,787.

Is medical marijuana still worth marketing in Michigan?

The state's own figures show medical facility sales of $311,726 in July 2026 against $270.5 million adult-use, with 52,115 registered patients and 87 provisioning centers. The patient base is real but small next to adult-use, and this page reports the state's numbers without speculating about the trend.
