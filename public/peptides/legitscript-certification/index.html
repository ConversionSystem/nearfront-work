LegitScript Certification, Explained for Peptide and Telehealth Businesses

By Guillermo Bravo · August 24, 2026 · 6 min read · ← Peptides library

One credential comes up whenever a health-adjacent business asks how to get back onto the big advertising platforms: LegitScript certification. Meta names it explicitly, payment processors read it, and most of the peptide category misunderstands what it is for. This article explains it from the primary sources, including the honest part about who it will not help.

What the certification actually is

LegitScript's own healthcare merchant certification guide, checked 24 August 2026, describes a third-party review of a healthcare business: what certification is, why it matters for working with major advertising platforms and payment processors, who is eligible, and the documentation required before applying. The certification is a standing status, monitored rather than granted once, and it exists because platforms and processors do not want to adjudicate healthcare legitimacy themselves.

The clearest platform hook is Meta's. Meta's drugs and pharmaceuticals standard limits prescription drug promotion to online pharmacies and telehealth providers that are actively certified with LegitScript and have applied to Meta for permission, with ads restricted to the United States, Canada and New Zealand and to adults. Google's healthcare policies run equivalent certification gates for pharmacy and telehealth promotion. In both cases the platform outsources the trust question, and the certification is the answer it accepts.

The part most peptide businesses skip past

Certification is built for regulated healthcare models: licensed pharmacies, telehealth practices operating within prescribing rules, and adjacent services that can document their compliance. Whether any specific business qualifies is LegitScript's determination against its published standards, and nobody outside that review, including us, can promise an outcome.

What can be said plainly is directional. A telehealth clinic working with licensed prescribers and a compounding pharmacy on lawful products has a certification story to assemble. A seller of unapproved substances labeled for research does not, because the certification exists precisely to separate documented, regulated healthcare from everything else. For that half of the category, the paid platforms are not one credential away; they are closed, as our ads-policy article documents from the platforms' own text.

Why it matters beyond advertising

The same certification is read by payment processors, and LegitScript's guide frames it that way: advertising platforms and payment processors together. Merchant risk teams treat healthcare as a high-risk category, and a business that cannot document its compliance posture pays for it in processing relationships even if it never runs an ad. Certification is one of the few portable artifacts that answers the question for multiple gatekeepers at once.

For a certifiable business, the practical sequence is unglamorous: get the regulatory house in order first, assemble the documentation LegitScript's guide describes, apply, and only then plan the ad program that certification unlocks. Marketing built in the other order tends to end in the account suspensions the platforms' policies promise.

LegitScript certification is the credential Meta names for prescription promotion and the one payment processors read, per both organizations' own documents. It is built for documented, regulated healthcare models, its outcome is LegitScript's call alone, and for sellers of unapproved research-labeled substances it is not a path onto the platforms at all.

Our role in this is narrow and worth stating: we are a search firm, not a certification consultancy, and nothing here is legal advice. Where certification is realistic, we build the organic and branded-search foundation that makes the eventual ad spend land on a site worth trusting. Where it is not, organic visibility is not the fallback plan. It is the only plan, and it deserves to be built like one.

Frequently asked questions

What is LegitScript certification?

A third-party certification of healthcare merchants that, per LegitScript's own guide, matters for working with major advertising platforms and payment processors. It reviews eligibility and documentation and functions as standing, monitored status rather than a one-time badge.

Do you need LegitScript certification to advertise peptides on Meta?

Meta's advertising standard limits prescription drug promotion to online pharmacies and telehealth providers actively certified with LegitScript that have also obtained Meta's written permission, restricted to the United States, Canada and New Zealand. Businesses outside those models are not eligible for that pathway, and unsafe-substance restrictions apply at Meta's discretion regardless.

Can a research peptide seller get LegitScript certified?

Certification decisions belong to LegitScript, but its healthcare merchant program is built for documented, regulated models such as licensed pharmacies and telehealth practices. A seller of unapproved substances labeled for research is not what the program describes, and no certification consultant can honestly promise otherwise.

Is LegitScript only about advertising?

No. LegitScript's guide frames certification as mattering to payment processors as well as ad platforms. Healthcare is a high-risk merchant category, and the same documentation that satisfies an ad platform's gate is read by processing and banking partners.
