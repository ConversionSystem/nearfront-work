Thin for its size

How many dispensaries are in Miami?

Nineteen inside the city, and 39 across Miami-Dade County once Miami Beach's six, North Miami Beach's four, North Miami's three and a scattering of others are included.

Set that against the rest of the state and it stands out. Palm Beach County has 56 dispensing locations, Pinellas 54, Hillsborough 52, Orange 51, Lee 43 and Duval 40, all ahead of Miami-Dade's 39. Orlando alone, one city, has 43 locations, more than the whole of Miami-Dade County.

Florida publishes the addresses but nothing about why the distribution looks like this, so we will not offer a theory. What is verifiable is the consequence: each Miami-Dade location covers more ground and more people than its equivalent in Tampa Bay or Central Florida, which raises the value of appearing in searches from across that wider catchment.

In short: Miami has 19 medical cannabis dispensing locations and Miami-Dade County 39. That is fewer than Palm Beach (56), Pinellas (54), Hillsborough (52), Orange (51), Lee (43) and Duval (40), and fewer than the city of Orlando alone, which has 43.

A wider catchment

What does a thin footprint mean for a Miami dispensary?

It means your realistic service area is larger than a store map suggests, and so is the search territory worth competing for. Where a Tampa patient may have several options within a few minutes, a Miami-Dade patient more often has a meaningful drive in front of them, and the decision is made earlier, in a search, rather than by walking past a storefront.

The operator field is also unusually even here. Trulieve holds three of the city's 19 locations, Cookies two, Surterra two and Curaleaf two, with Goldflower, Jungle Boys and others holding one apiece. Statewide, Trulieve runs 170 locations and the top four operators hold 51 percent of everything, so Miami is one of the few places where that weight is not decisive.

Both facts point the same way. A store that ranks across the neighborhoods and municipalities it can realistically serve, rather than only for its own address, is capturing trips that would otherwise go to a competitor several miles away for no reason other than visibility.

In short: With 39 locations across Miami-Dade against 56 in Palm Beach and 54 in Pinellas, each Miami-area dispensary serves a wider catchment, and the choice is usually made in a search before the drive. The local operator field is also unusually even: no company holds more than three of the city's 19 locations.

The medical layer

What kind of customer is searching in Miami?

A registered one. Florida runs a medical-only cannabis program, and 939,639 people held an active state ID card as of 21 August 2026. Nobody walks in on impulse; every purchase follows a physician entry into the state registry and a card application.

That gives Miami dispensaries an audience with predictable needs and a renewal cycle, and it puts real weight on practical content. Which locations carry which product forms, what a renewal requires, what to bring on a first visit. These are searched, answerable from public documents, and almost nobody publishes them properly.

It also raises the cost of a stale listing. A patient who drives twenty minutes on wrong hours or outdated stock information does not usually give the store a second attempt, and in a county with fewer locations per person, that patient has probably already passed a competitor on the way.

In short: Florida is medical only, with 939,639 patients holding active ID cards statewide as of August 2026. Miami's searchers are registered repeat customers with renewal cycles, so accurate hours, stock detail and clear answers to card and renewal questions carry unusual weight in a county with relatively few locations.
