Watch the rate

What is the cannabis tax rate in Maryland, and why do sources disagree?

The answer is 12 percent. The sales and use tax on adult-use cannabis rose from 9 percent on 1 July 2025 under the Budget Reconciliation and Financing Act of 2025, and the Comptroller's quarterly revenue releases describe the July to September 2025 quarter as the first full quarter at the higher rate.

Here is the part worth knowing if you are researching this yourself: the Comptroller's own standing cannabis page still displays 9 percent. An official state page, on the taxing authority's own domain, publishing a rate its own press releases superseded a year earlier. We found it while sourcing this page, and it is a useful illustration of why any cannabis statistic without a date attached should be treated as a rumor.

This matters commercially, not just editorially. Content that quotes stale tax figures is content a well-informed operator stops trusting, and increasingly it is content AI answer engines will contradict when they cite a fresher source.

In short: Maryland's adult-use cannabis sales and use tax is 12 percent, effective 1 July 2025 under the Budget Reconciliation and Financing Act of 2025. The Comptroller's static cannabis page still shows the superseded 9 percent rate, so cite the quarterly revenue reports instead.

Market age

How does a young market change the SEO opportunity?

Maryland began adult-use sales on 1 July 2023. Set against Oregon or Colorado, where retail has run for a decade, that is a market still deciding who its incumbents are.

Consumer search behavior follows a fairly consistent arc in new adult-use states. Early queries are informational and anxious: what is legal, how much can I buy, do I need identification, is this dispensary legitimate. Over time the mix shifts toward navigational and transactional searches: a store name, a product category, a delivery question. The brands that answered the early questions well are usually the ones that own the branded searches later, because they were the source that resolved someone's uncertainty the first time.

Practically, that means Maryland rewards content most other agencies consider beneath them. A clear, current explainer on purchase limits or identification requirements is not thought leadership. It is the page that earns a first-time buyer, and in a market at this stage the first-time buyer is most of the market.

In short: Maryland's adult-use market opened on 1 July 2023, so search demand still skews toward first-time-buyer questions. Dispensaries that answer those questions accurately tend to capture the branded and transactional searches those same buyers make later.

New markets generate exactly the definitional questions AI assistants answer directly. Being the source those answers draw on is the compounding version of this work.

Measurement

What should a Maryland dispensary actually measure?

Most dispensary reporting stops at a rank number and a traffic chart. Neither tells an operator whether the money worked, and in a market still finding its shape that gap is expensive, because you cannot tell an early lead from noise.

Three things are worth instrumenting properly. The first is position across the trade area rather than at the storefront, because distance is a ranking input and a single number averages away the neighborhoods where you are absent. In the Baltimore to Washington corridor, where trade areas overlap heavily, that average is particularly misleading.

The second is the action, not the visit. Direction requests, calls, and menu clicks are the events that precede revenue, and they are measurable. A dispensary that grew sessions 40 percent while direction requests stayed flat has bought attention from people who were never going to drive over.

The third is attribution back to the point of sale. Maryland's public quarterly tax data tells you what the state market did; only your own numbers tell you what your search work did. Connecting menu and pre-order data to the visibility work is what turns a monthly report into a decision about where next quarter's effort goes.

In short: measure position across the whole trade area rather than at the storefront, count actions like direction requests and calls rather than sessions, and connect both back to point-of-sale data. Rankings without revenue attribution are a screenshot.

FAQ

Common questions about cannabis SEO in Maryland

What is the cannabis tax rate in Maryland?

12 percent. The sales and use tax on adult-use cannabis rose from 9 percent to 12 percent on July 1, 2025 under the Budget Reconciliation and Financing Act of 2025. Worth knowing: the Comptroller's own static cannabis page still displays the old 9 percent figure, while that office's quarterly revenue reports confirm the 12 percent rate.

When did adult-use cannabis sales begin in Maryland?

July 1, 2023, when the cannabis sales and use tax took effect. That makes Maryland a young adult-use market by comparison with the West Coast, where retail has operated for a decade or more, and buyer habits in Maryland are still being formed rather than defended.

How much cannabis tax revenue does Maryland collect?

Maryland collected 26.2 million dollars in adult-use cannabis sales and use tax between January and March 2026, according to the Comptroller of Maryland. Quarterly collections give a public, reliable read on the size and direction of the state market.

Can Maryland dispensaries run Google Ads?

No. Google Ads prohibits promoting THC products to United States consumers, and Meta prohibits them as well. Maryland dispensaries compete for the same organic local visibility as every other market, with no paid shortcut available to anyone.

Does Maryland let counties ban dispensaries?

Maryland localities generally regulate cannabis retail through zoning rather than the outright municipal bans common in California. The practical distinction matters for site selection and for how a trade area is defined, and the specific rules vary by jurisdiction, so confirm the current position with the county or municipality before relying on it.
