The gap that is costing you the map pack

Share of Local Voice (SoLV) is the percentage of the grid where a location shows up in the local 3-pack for a given search. Average Rank Position (ARP) is where it lands when it does show up.

Key finding

The three listings beating you on "dispensary near me" have one real review between them

Mary Mart carries a 4.8-star rating across 3,362 reviews, the deepest review profile of any cannabis retailer in this market, and still lands at an average rank of 9.4 on "dispensary near me," outside the 3-pack across most of the grid. What is beating you there is not real competition: Hyde Recreational Cannabis Store (1.0 stars, 1 review, 60% SoLV), Green Shop Recreational Cannabis Store (0 reviews, 40% SoLV), and Weed And Cannabis Delivery Dispensary (0 reviews, 32% SoLV). This is a Google Business Profile integrity problem, not a demand or reputation problem, and it is usually one of the fastest wins available: spam reporting plus profile hardening, not a content rebuild.

Beyond the radius

You are not ranking outside your immediate radius, and the map grid will not fix that on its own

The scan above is the real story: Mary Mart shows up close to the store and falls off fast past a mile or two. AI visibility: Google AI Overviews, Gemini, ChatGPT, Google AI Mode

Share of AI Voice (SAIV) is how often your business is mentioned in AI-generated answers across the scanned grid. Average Rank Position (ARP) is where you land in that answer when you are mentioned. Same 9x9 grid, 2.5-mile radius, same two keywords.

Key finding

Google Maps buries you behind spam, and AI answer engines are a mixed picture

ChatGPT mentions Mary Mart in 64% to 72% of answers across the grid for these two searches, at an average position of 2.5 to 3.0. Google AI Mode mentions you in 43% to 57% of answers, and Gemini in 40% to 44%, both landing you inside the top two results when you show up. All three sit well ahead of the Google Maps numbers above, and customers asking those tools where to buy cannabis in Tacoma are already being pointed to you more often than not. Google AI Overviews is the outlier: it mentions you in only 4% of answers for "dispensary" and does not mention you at all for "dispensary near me," the same near-zero pattern the Maps scan shows. That gap matters because AI Overviews sits directly inside Google's own search results, the highest-traffic surface of the four. The campaign's job on the AI side is to protect and extend the lead already built on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Mode, while treating Google AI Overviews as a build-from-near-zero problem much like the Maps pack.

From your last call: site and AI-discoverability updates

A few items from your September 15 call with Guillermo Bravo and Misha Jones came up outside the map and AI-visibility scans above. They are not priced into the plan above yet, flagged here so nothing gets lost before the scope is finalized.

AI text file

An LLM sitemap is worth doing, but it will not replace the review and authority work above

An llms.txt-style file gives AI crawlers like ChatGPT and Gemini a clean, structured summary of Mary Mart, its offerings, and its key pages to read directly, instead of piecing it together from the site. It is a low-lift, one-time build, and Nearfront can put it in place as part of this engagement. On its own it moves the needle less than the review volume and off-site citation work covered above, so it is best treated as a small addition alongside the core plan rather than the headline tactic.

Homepage restructuring

Move the live product menu, deals, and best-sellers onto the homepage itself, ahead of older content, so the highest-traffic page on the site sells first.

Neighborhood pages

Dedicated, front-facing landing pages for Proctor District, central Tacoma, and nearby neighborhoods, to catch the searches happening outside the immediate radius shown in the map scan above.

Blog content plan

Posts built around your top five revenue-driving brands and current deals, refreshed every 90 days to stay indexed and relevant.

Menu widget embeds

Jane shopping widgets embedded on the homepage and deals page, with deal images added across the site, so browsing and buying happen without leaving the page.

Headless site architecture

A larger option: rebuild the site so the product menu is the site itself, letting every product, brand, and category compete individually in search instead of only the homepage.
