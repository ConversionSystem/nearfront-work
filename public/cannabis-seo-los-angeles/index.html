The ratio that matters

How many Los Angeles County cities allow dispensaries?

Twenty. Los Angeles County contains 88 incorporated cities, and the state's ordinance file records 67 of them as prohibiting storefront cannabis retail. One more, Huntington Park, permits medical sales only. That leaves twenty cities where an adult-use storefront can lawfully operate, among them Los Angeles, Long Beach, Pasadena, Santa Monica, Culver City and West Hollywood.

Unincorporated Los Angeles County, which covers a large share of the county's land and includes substantial residential areas, prohibits every commercial cannabis activity. So the closed portion of the county is larger than the city list alone suggests.

Put in population terms, our sum of the 67 prohibiting cities comes to roughly 3.2 million residents, before counting anyone in unincorporated territory. Those people are not outside the market. They are inside it, and they have to leave home to buy legally.

In short: Los Angeles County has 88 incorporated cities. Sixty-seven prohibit storefront cannabis retail, one permits medical sales only, and twenty allow adult-use storefronts, including Los Angeles, Long Beach, Pasadena and Santa Monica. Unincorporated Los Angeles County prohibits all commercial cannabis activity.

Who you actually compete with

Who is a Los Angeles dispensary really competing against?

Not the county. Nineteen other cities. When most of the surrounding map is closed by ordinance, the businesses that can take your customer are the ones inside the same short list of permitted jurisdictions, and they are often nowhere near you geographically.

This inverts the usual local SEO instinct. In an ordinary retail category, a competitor eight miles away is barely a competitor. Here, a store in a permitted city eight miles off may be the nearest legal alternative for an entire population that lives between you, because nothing legal can open in the gap. Distance stops being a proxy for competition and permission takes its place.

The corollary is more useful than the warning. Every closed city near you is a place where no rival can ever plant a location, no matter how much they spend. Ranking for those communities is a position no competitor can dislodge by opening nearer to the customer, because opening nearer is illegal.

In short: A Los Angeles dispensary competes with the nineteen other Los Angeles County cities that permit storefront retail, not with the county at large. Because no competitor may open in the 67 prohibiting cities, rankings earned in those communities cannot be undercut by a rival moving closer.

Working the map

How should a Los Angeles dispensary choose which neighborhoods to target?

Start by separating the map into three layers rather than one. The first is your own city, where you compete with every other permitted Los Angeles storefront and where the queries are the most contested in the county. The second is the closed ring, the prohibiting cities within a realistic drive, where demand is real and competition is structurally thin. The third is the other permitted cities, where you are the outsider and the economics rarely justify a fight.

Most operators collapse all three into a single push for citywide visibility, which spends the budget in the most crowded layer and ignores the cheapest one. Los Angeles is large enough that citywide ranking is not even a coherent goal: the city stretches from the harbor to the valley, and a customer in one part of it will never travel to the other.

Work the neighborhoods and the closed ring together. Within Los Angeles, name the districts people actually use, because that is how they search and how they describe where they are. Outside it, build content and profile signals for the specific prohibiting communities in your catchment, and measure position for each one separately rather than averaging them into a single citywide number that hides both the wins and the gaps.

In short: Split the map into your own city, the closed ring of prohibiting cities nearby, and the other permitted cities. The closed ring carries real demand and structurally thin competition, and it is the layer most Los Angeles operators skip while spending on contested citywide terms.

Los Angeles, for context

Los Angeles is governed by the City of Los Angeles, with cannabis licensing administered by the Department of Cannabis Regulation and business support through the Los Angeles Area Chamber of Commerce. Its commercial districts run through Downtown, Hollywood, Silver Lake, Venice, Studio City and the wider San Fernando Valley, near destinations including the Los Angeles County Museum of Art and the Griffith Observatory. Angelenos name their neighborhood, not their city, and their searches follow.

What is the cannabis excise tax in Los Angeles?

California charges 15 percent of gross receipts on retail sales, effective 1 October 2025. The rate had risen to 19 percent on 1 July 2025 before Assembly Bill 564 restored 15 percent three months later. Local business taxes are set separately by the city, so confirm the current Los Angeles rate with the city directly.
