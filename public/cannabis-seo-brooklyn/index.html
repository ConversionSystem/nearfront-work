The number that matters

How many dispensaries are actually open in Brooklyn?

Eighty-two. Not 145, which is the license count almost every article quotes, because 63 of those licenses belong to businesses that are not trading. New York's licensing data carries an operational status field, and it separates a permit from a shop with a door someone can walk through.

The distinction is worth more than trivia. If you plan against 145 competitors you will conclude Brooklyn is impossibly crowded and behave defensively. If you plan against 82, and understand that they are clustered rather than evenly spread, the picture changes: in most Brooklyn neighborhoods your genuine competitive set is a handful of stores, several of which have an incomplete Google Business Profile and no review system.

Brooklyn also converts licenses into open stores less often than Manhattan does. Manhattan runs 95 active from 142 licenses, a 67 percent activation rate; Brooklyn manages 57 percent. Brooklyn leads the state on licenses issued and trails Manhattan on stores actually trading.

In short: Brooklyn holds 145 retail dispensary licenses but only 82 are operationally active per the Office of Cannabis Management, an activation rate of 57 percent against Manhattan's 67. Your real Map Pack competition is the 82, not the 145.

Brooklyn, for context

Brooklyn is New York City's most populous borough, governed through the Brooklyn Borough President's office and served by the Brooklyn Chamber of Commerce. Its retail corridors run along Bedford Avenue, Flatbush Avenue, and Fulton Street, anchored by destinations including the Brooklyn Museum, Brooklyn Botanic Garden, and Barclays Center. Customers here navigate by neighborhood and subway stop rather than street address, and their searches reflect that.
