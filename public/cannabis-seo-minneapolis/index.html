The newest market in the country

How new is the Minneapolis cannabis market?

New enough that the state's own paperwork captures the starting gun. Minnesota's 2025 Local Government Cannabis Aid was cancelled because the Office of Cannabis Management certified zero licensed cannabis businesses as of January 1, 2025; licensing began that June, and the first license in the OCM roster is dated June 18, 2025. Every cannabis business license in Minnesota is younger than that.

By July 6, 2026, the roster held 313 licensed businesses statewide: 219 microbusinesses, 19 medical combination licenses, 17 mezzobusinesses, 14 standalone retail licenses, plus cultivators, wholesalers, transporters and the rest. Minneapolis leads every city with 24 licensed businesses, edging Saint Paul's 23, with 19 of the 24 operating licensed retail sites, names like Green Goods on 9th Street, Legacy Cannabis on Lyndale and Watchtower on University Avenue.

One layer sits underneath all of this: 253 registered hemp-derived edible retailers already operate in Minneapolis, a hangover of Minnesota's unusual 2022 hemp law. They are a different registration entirely, but they trained a customer base and crowded the shelf before the first dispensary opened. A Minneapolis cannabis license does not enter a naive market, it enters a noisy one.

The tax math

What do Minnesota's cannabis taxes mean for a Minneapolis store?

Minnesota charges a 15 percent cannabis gross receipts tax on top of the 6.875 percent state sales tax and any local taxes, per the Department of Revenue's cannabis tax page. The rate began at 10 percent in the 2023 law and was raised to 15. Every dollar of demand a store wins therefore carries one of the heavier state tax loads in the adult-use map.

The collections history doubles as a market history. The state's tax handbook records $11.6 million of cannabis gross receipts tax in fiscal 2024, at the old 10 percent rate, before a single dispensary existed. That entire figure is the hemp-derived edible layer, which is exactly why the 253 registered hemp retailers in Minneapolis matter to a dispensary's competitive picture: they were paying cannabis taxes before cannabis stores existed.

Heavy tax plus a crowded lower-potency shelf pushes the competitive question to value and convenience, and both of those are search problems. The customer deciding between a dispensary, a hemp shop and a longer drive to a suburb resolves it in a search box. Appearing there, with accurate hours, menu and reviews, is the cheapest lever a Minneapolis operator controls.

In short: Minnesota levies a 15 percent cannabis gross receipts tax plus 6.875 percent state sales tax and local taxes. FY2024 collections of $11.6 million predate dispensaries entirely, coming from the hemp-derived layer that still operates 253 registered retailers in Minneapolis and competes for the same customers.
