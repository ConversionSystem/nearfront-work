The scale of it

Is Portland the most competitive cannabis market in the country?

It is a serious candidate, and the comparison that makes it clearest is with the pages next door. Brooklyn holds 145 cannabis retail licenses across a borough of roughly 2.7 million people. Portland holds 231 across a city of roughly 650,000. Portland has more licensed cannabis retail than Brooklyn in about a quarter of the population.

Then apply the constraint that defines the category: Google Ads prohibits promoting THC products to United States consumers, and Meta does the same. Not one of those 231 Portland businesses can buy its way to visibility. In an ordinary sector this much competition would produce an advertising arms race. Here it produces a search race, and the prize is the three slots in the Map Pack.

Statewide, 795 licenses have produced 678 retailers actually recording sales, so the license count overstates the live competition here as it does everywhere. But even discounted, Portland is a market where the difference between ranking and not ranking is most of the business.

In short: Portland holds 231 cannabis retail licenses, 29 percent of Oregon's 795, in a city of roughly 650,000 people. Brooklyn, with four times the population, has 145. No Oregon dispensary can run paid search or social for THC, so all of that competition resolves into organic local search.

Boundaries

Which parts of the Portland metro are separate markets?

This matters more here than most operators expect, because the metro's municipal boundaries do not match how people talk about the area. Gresham and Troutdale are separate cities inside Multnomah County, with 6 and 1 retail licenses respectively, and neither is part of Portland's 231. A Gresham store competing on Portland-wide terms is competing in a market it does not sit in.

Portland itself crosses three counties. Most of it, and 222 of its licenses, lie in Multnomah, but the city extends into Washington and Clackamas counties, and the remaining licenses sit there. County-level data and city-level data therefore tell different stories, and mixing them is how a competitive analysis ends up wrong.

The practical version: define your trade area by drive time and neighborhood, never by city limits or county lines. A store on the eastern edge of Portland competes with Gresham businesses that no Portland-wide report will ever show it.

In short: Gresham (6 licenses) and Troutdale (1) are separate cities in Multnomah County and are not part of Portland's 231. Portland spans three counties, with 222 licenses in Multnomah and the rest in the Washington and Clackamas portions, so county and city figures are not interchangeable.

Portland, for context

Portland is governed by the City of Portland and served by the Portland Metro Chamber. Its retail districts run along Hawthorne, Alberta, Division, and Mississippi, near destinations including Powell's City of Books, Washington Park, and the Portland Art Museum. What is the cannabis tax rate in Portland?

Oregon charges 17 percent state retail tax on recreational marijuana, and cities and counties may add up to 3 percent more with voter approval, so a Portland buyer can face up to 20 percent. There is no separate Portland cannabis tax beyond that local option.
